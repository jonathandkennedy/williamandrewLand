Riders get blamed first. That is the fight.

Motorcycle claims are not car claims with a smaller vehicle. The injuries are worse, the coverage is thinner, and the bias starts before anyone looks at the evidence.

The bias is real and it is early

Adjusters and jurors arrive assuming the rider was speeding or weaving. Nothing in the file corrects that on its own — it takes scene evidence, sightline analysis and often a reconstruction.

Left-turn cases turn on sightlines

The most common serious motorcycle crash is a car turning left across a rider's path. "I never saw him" is not a defence, but it becomes one if nobody documents what the driver could actually see.

Injuries are graver and slower

Fractures, road rash, degloving and head injuries need months before anyone can value them. An early settlement almost always closes the claim below what the treatment ends up costing.

Gear gets used against you

Utah does not require a helmet over 21, and not wearing one is not negligence in itself. Expect the argument anyway, and expect it applied to your gear, your exhaust and your bike's appearance.

Coverage runs out fast

Serious rider injuries routinely exceed the at-fault driver's policy. Finding UM/UIM, household policies and any employer coverage is often the difference between a policy limit and the real number.

Utah splits the fault

Utah reduces recovery by your share of the blame and bars it at 50%. On a bike, the fault argument is where most of the money is won or lost.

What we do in the first 72 hours

Bike cases are won on scene evidence that stops existing within days.

  1. Preserve the bike before it is scrapped

    The bike itself is evidence — damage pattern, gouge marks, final rest position. Once an insurer totals it and sends it to salvage, that is gone. We move first.

  2. Photograph sightlines from the driver's seat

    In a left-turn case, what the driver could see from where they sat is often the whole case. That has to be documented before landscaping, signage or seasonal light changes.

  3. Find the cameras

    Intersection, business and doorbell footage is overwritten in days. We canvass and send preservation letters immediately.

  4. Take the adjuster calls

    From the day we are hired, the other insurer talks to us. No recorded statement while you are medicated and no signing a blanket medical release.

  5. Get you to doctors who treat riders

    Orthopaedic and wound care that documents properly, plus the head-injury screening that routine ER visits often skip.

Straight answers

The questions people actually ask on the first call.

I wasn't wearing a helmet. Does that end my claim?

No. Utah requires a helmet only under 21, and not wearing one is not negligence by itself. The insurer will still raise it, and it can affect head-injury damages specifically, but it does not bar a claim for the rest — and it has nothing to do with who caused the crash.

The driver says they never saw me. Is that a defence?

No — a driver turning across traffic has to make sure the way is clear. "I never saw him" is an admission of failing to look, not an excuse. But it only lands that way if someone documents the sightlines while they still look the way they did that day.

What if the crash was partly my fault?

Call anyway. Utah reduces recovery by your share of the fault rather than eliminating it, and you can still recover while your share stays under 50%. Riders routinely blame themselves for things that were not theirs to control.

Does my immigration status affect my case?

No. Anyone injured by someone else's negligence in Utah can bring a claim, whatever their immigration status. No Social Security number or driver's licence is required. What you tell us is confidential and we do not report to immigration.

What does this cost me?

Nothing up front and nothing unless we win. The fee is a percentage of what we recover, agreed in writing before we start, and case costs come out of the recovery rather than your pocket.

In Utah you generally have four years from the date of a crash to file an injury claim, and two years for a wrongful death claim. If a city, county or state vehicle was involved, a formal notice of claim can be due within one year — far sooner than most people expect. Deadlines turn on the facts, so confirm yours rather than assuming.
